# DIEZ's Own Emotional Format (from his posted, proven Story scripts)

This is Diez's PERSONAL emotional-storytelling format, reverse-engineered directly from the football stories he has actually posted and that performed (Luis Díaz, Modrić, Iniesta, Andrés Escobar, Cape Verde, Memo Ochoa, and more). When writing an Emotional Storyteller script, this format is blended 50/50 with the base viral style — and where they differ, THIS document (Diez's real habits) wins, because these are his own scripts.

---

## 1. THE HOOK — a flash-forward, almost always "Imagine…"

Diez opens ~90% of his stories with a 2–3 line **"Imagine" flash-forward** that jumps to the emotional peak before telling the story. The final line is the turn and lands the paradox. Real openings:

- "Imagine watching men take your father away into the jungle / You don't know if he is alive or if he is ever coming back / And years later, you score in your country's World Cup opener — for the man they tried to take from you." (Luis Díaz)
- "Imagine a boy sleeping in a refugee hotel / you hear bombs dropping outside that have already buried your grandfather / But years later, he would carry a nation smaller than most cities to the edge of glory." (Modrić)
- "Imagine you are the most iconic goalkeeper in the world / At forty years old, they call you finished and forget your name / but then you join an immortal list of three — Messi, Ronaldo, and a goalkeeper the world begged to forget." (Ochoa)
- "Imagine the 116th minute of a World Cup final / Ninety years of waiting on the shoulders of a man who is playing for the best friend he buried with his own hands." (Iniesta — 2 lines)

Rules: second person ("Imagine you are…", "Imagine watching…", "Imagine being…"), 2 or 3 short lines, final line begins with **And / But** and reveals the twist. Do NOT name the subject in the hook.

**Underdog / nation variant** (used for country stories, no "Imagine"): open on the stage with a wry deflation, e.g. "The World Cup has its group of death. Spain and Uruguay — two world champion nations — will fight it out for a place in the knockouts. Oh… and Cape Verde. The fourth nation in the group." Use this shape for small-nation fairy tales.

## 2. THE ARC (his real beat order)

1. **Hook** (above).
2. **Origin / where they came from** — "[Name] came from [place], a forgotten corner of [country] that teaches you what it's like to grow up with nothing." Poverty, being overlooked, undernourished, rejected. Establish the underdog. Often a believer (a father who coaches on a dirt pitch, a mother, a friend).
3. **The rise** — the chance arrives, kept brief ("gets his chance at Porto in 2019, he moves to Europe like a boy jumping into darkness in search of a dream").
4. **The wound / the turn** — a DATE stated plainly on its own line ("October 28th, 2023." / "June 18th, 1994." / "the 17th of June, 2014."), then the trauma/injustice told cinematically and specifically.
5. **The dark middle** — the aftermath, the doubt, the years of waiting, the world counting them out.
6. **The World Cup moment** — a NEW date, the stadium, the crowd counted ("Eighty thousand inside the stadium"), a nation holding its breath, the anthem "sung through tears."
7. **The ceremonial full name** — at the threshold of the peak, the full birth name like a coronation: "stands Luis Fernando Díaz Marulanda." / "Guillermo Ochoa Magaña." / "Luka Modrić Kovač." Reserve it for THIS moment.
8. **The climax** — a minute marker, present tense, a beat of suspension, then the decisive act. Write it FRESH each time (do not recycle stock lines).
9. **The dedication / sky-point** — points to the heavens, searches the stands, for the one lost or watching "from above."
10. **The aphoristic closer** — two lines, a universal-truth contrast (see §4).
11. **Optional bold reflective coda** (Iniesta pattern) — before the closer, a short bolded meditation widening the meaning ("Iniesta did not just win a World Cup. He placed an angel above the head of every Spanish player who would ever come after him…"). Use for the biggest, most mythic stories.
12. **Optional CTA** — "if you enjoyed this story and want more like this one, please make sure you're following."

Not every story ends in triumph — tragedies (Escobar) and defeats (Modrić losing in extra time) keep the same architecture but invert the ending into ache and meaning.

### 2b. STRUCTURAL VARIANTS (pick the one that fits the story — all read across his posted scripts)

- **The Redemption / Comeback** (Díaz, Ochoa, Jiménez): origin → wound → darkness → the World Cup moment → goal → dedication → "Some… / Very few…". The default.
- **The Tragedy / Broken Promise** (Roberto Baggio, Andrés Escobar): the hook flashes forward to the heartbreak ("the day fate finally gives you that chance, it breaks you forever"). Build the dream, then the cruel fall. Closer is a mournful aphorism ("Socrates died poisoned. But Baggio died on his feet." / "under a badge, there is also a beating heart.").
- **The Injury Carried Forward** (Ismaël Koné): triumph → the injury in an ordinary minute → the whole team's grief → he salutes the crowd from the stretcher → "Now eleven men carry it for him. Some dreams you finish yourself. Others, your brothers finish for you."
- **The Underdog Nation** (Cape Verde, Croatia 2018): open on the stage with a wry deflation or the "born from war / scattered as refugees" origin of a whole country → the impossible run, match by match → defeat-with-honour or coronation → "History remembers the champions. But it falls in love with the ones who were never supposed to be there." / "Some nations win the World Cup. And some, by refusing to break, win something history remembers even longer."
- **The Rivalry / Geopolitical** (England–Argentina Malvinas): the hook is a human cost of history → the match as the real battlefield → the iconic moment (Hand of God / Goal of the Century) framed as justice/meaning → tie the past to the present fixture → "Some rivalries aren't about football. Football is just the language two enemies use to keep speaking."
- **The Historic Match** (Maracanazo, Mineirazo, Casillas, Zidane): a dated, cinematic re-telling of a famous game as a tragedy or coronation, present tense at the decisive moment, poetic closer.
- **The Cause / Awareness** (Alphonso Davies, Jayden Adams, Gakpo): the redemption/tribute arc opens up at the end into a real-world message (mental health, one in four, a lost child) and a heartfelt SHARE CTA — "Please share this message with someone you love… It is okay not to be okay. Just don't carry it alone." Use only when the subject genuinely lived the cause.
- **The Historical Parallel / "History Rhymes"** (Spain Sound, England Norway, Yamal France, Malvinas): tell a famous PAST moment in full (2010 Spain, the 1981 Norway upset, Yamal's 2024 goal, Maradona '86) as its own mini-story, then pivot — "And now the wheel turns again. [Date]. [The current fixture]" — drawing the parallel between the legend and an upcoming match, so the past frames the present. Close on the rhyme: "History doesn't repeat itself. But sometimes, it rhymes." This is one of his most-used recent formats — reach for it whenever a current fixture echoes a historic one.

## 3. VOICE, FLOW & REGISTER

- **Flowing, complete sentences** chained with and / because / but / until — long lines that breathe, not choppy fragments. Reserve a short fragment only for a hammer beat.
- **Present tense at the peak** for immediacy ("The match kicks off and in the 65th the ball arrives. And Luis Díaz does the only thing he has ever known — he runs at the impossible.").
- **Religious / cosmic register**: heaven, angels, "ascends into the heaven where Diego was already waiting", "a nation daring to dream again", "touch heaven".
- **The believer & the doubt**: "the boy nobody bet on", "the man the world swore would never be enough", "scouts look at him once and turn away."
- **Specific facts are the spine**: real dates, clubs, scorelines, minutes, ages. Occasionally a polymarket odd even in a story ("a 1% chance of qualifying according to polymarket").

## 4. HIS SIGNATURE CLOSERS (two-line universal-truth contrasts)

He almost always ends on a "Some… / Very few…" or two-part contrast. Real closers:
- "Some boys are buried by where they were born. Very few dig themselves out — and fewer still come back to give their whole country a reason to fly." (Díaz)
- "Some men are born surrounded by death. Very few give a whole country a reason to live." (Modrić)
- "Some men score goals to be remembered. Andrés Iniesta scored one so that someone else would never be forgotten." (Iniesta)
- "Some men are remembered for how they arrive. Guillermo Ochoa will be remembered because, for twenty patient years, he refused to leave." (Ochoa)
- "History remembers the champions. But it falls in love with the ones who were never supposed to be there." (Cape Verde)
- "Some nations win the World Cup. And some, by refusing to break, win something history remembers even longer." (Croatia 2018 — defeat with honour)
- "Some dreams you finish yourself. Others, your brothers finish for you." (Koné — injury carried forward)
- "Some rivalries aren't about football. Football is just the language two enemies use to keep speaking." (Malvinas — rivalry)
- "Socrates died poisoned. But Baggio died on his feet." (Baggio — tragedy)
- The moral sting (tragedies): "he lost it because two men forgot that under a badge, there is also a beating heart." (Escobar)
- The cause/awareness close (only when the subject raised it): "It is okay not to be okay. Just don't carry it alone." (Davies)
- "History doesn't repeat itself. But sometimes, it rhymes." (Spain Sound — historical parallel)
- "Empires build monuments to be remembered. Underdogs only need one sentence to never be forgotten." (England Norway)
- "Some players spend an entire career proving the doubters wrong. Others were born to make them forget why they ever doubted them." (Yamal France)
- "Some men score to be remembered. Cody Gakpo scored so his unborn son would never be forgotten." (Gakpo — the reusable "Some men score to be remembered / [name] scored so… would never be forgotten" template)

The dominant shape is a two-line contrast — "Some… / Very few…", "Some… / And some…", "Some… / Others…", or "History remembers… / But it falls in love with…". Build it from the specific story's own central image — never a generic line.

## 5. RECURRING MOTIFS (techniques — reinvent the wording each time)

- "under a badge, there is also a beating heart" (used for tragedy/injustice)
- "the boy the world swore would never be enough" / "the boy from [place]"
- ascending "into the heaven where [lost one] was already waiting"
- points to the heavens / "his father weeps from above"
- "a nation that has buried too many sons, daring to dream again"
- the wound and the redemption sharing the same act ("the same header that broke him is the header that finally crowned him")

## 6. LENGTH

~350–550 words (~2–2.5 min). His biggest stories (Iniesta, Ochoa) run longer with a reflective coda; tighter ones sit around 350.

## 7. WHAT MAKES IT *DIEZ* (vs the base style)

- The **"Imagine…" hook is near-mandatory** for player stories — lead with it.
- The closer is a **"Some… / Very few…" contrast** built from the story's image.
- The **"under a badge there is a beating heart"** moral for tragedies.
- The optional **bold reflective coda** on the biggest stories.
- Real dates on their own lines, ceremonial full name at the peak, and a willingness to reference **polymarket odds** even inside an emotional story.
