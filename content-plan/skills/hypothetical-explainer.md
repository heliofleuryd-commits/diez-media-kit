# Format Skill: What-If / Hypothetical Scenario Explainer

# Format Skill File: The "What-If / Hypothetical Scenario Explainer"

## Overview
This format takes a speculative premise — a counterfactual, a thought experiment, or a "what would happen if" question — and treats it with the seriousness of a documented fact. The power comes from the presenter committing fully to the hypothetical, building it brick by brick with real-world evidence, and arriving at a confident verdict. The viewer is invited to play the game with the creator, not just watch it.

---

## 1. Structural Pattern (The Arc)

**Hook (Bold Hypothetical) → Credibility Anchor → Construction Phase → Verdict → CTA**

- **Hook — The Provocative Premise:** Open with the "what if" question or a counterintuitive claim delivered straight to camera. It should feel borderline absurd or impossible so the viewer leans in to find out *how* it could be true.
- **Credibility Anchor:** Immediately ground the fantasy in something real — a historical parallel, an official proposal, a confirmed rule, a real data point. This earns the right to speculate. ("Hear me out... 10 years ago this exact thing happened.")
- **Construction Phase:** Methodically build the scenario. List the components (clubs, players, conditions), weigh trade-offs out loud, and reason through each choice with a clear POV. This is the body — the presenter *guides*, never just lists.
- **Verdict / Payoff:** Deliver a confident, opinionated conclusion. Don't sit on the fence. Give a realistic prediction, a ranking, a "this could challenge X" statement.
- **CTA:** Invite the audience into the next hypothetical — "comment which one I should do next" — or pivot to follow/sponsor.

---

## 2. Pacing Template (Timestamps)

| Time | Beat | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| **0–4s** | Hook | The bold "what if" question or impossible claim, straight to camera |
| **4–12s** | Credibility anchor | The real-world hook that makes the fantasy plausible — silence/music lets it land |
| **12–45s** | Construction | Build the scenario piece by piece, reasoning aloud with strong opinions |
| **45–60s** | Verdict | Confident conclusion + comparison/ranking that crowns the payoff |
| **60–70s** | CTA | "Which one next?" or follow prompt / sponsor handoff |

*Note: For shorter cuts (30–40s), compress construction to 3–4 key beats. The hook and verdict are non-negotiable.*

---

## 3. Visual Rhythm

- **On-camera / b-roll split:** Roughly 50/50. Presenter delivers hook and verdict to camera; construction phase rides over b-roll (match footage, maps, club crests, table graphics).
- **Cut frequency:** Moderate, cinematic — one cut every 3–5 seconds. Let the hook breathe with a held shot before the first cut.
- **Text overlays:** Use sparingly to anchor key data — names, market values, league positions, years. Overlays should reinforce, not narrate. Clean sans-serif, bottom-third or beside the relevant b-roll.
- **B-roll pattern:** Every clip must illustrate the exact thing being said in that beat (name a club → show that club's footage/crest). For "build a league" variants, a growing map or accumulating logo grid creates visual momentum.
- **Music:** Builds subtly under construction, swells at the verdict. Drop to near-silence for the hook to create gravity.

---

## 4. When To Use This Format

- **Counterfactuals about the present:** "Can this team still win X despite Y?" — fed by current controversy or crisis.
- **Geographic / structural thought experiments:** Merged leagues, redrawn competitions, alternate formats.
- **Squad-building hypotheticals:** "If club X spent Y, what's the XI?" / "Can nation Z win the World Cup?"
- **Financial / consequence speculation:** "If they get relegated, what happens to wages/contracts?"
- **Best moments:** Mid-season uncertainty, transfer windows, post-sacking chaos, pre-tournament hype — any time the *future is genuinely unknown* and fans are already arguing about it.

---

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Neutral fence-sitting:** Refusing to deliver a verdict kills the payoff. The whole format demands the presenter commits to an opinion.
- **Weak credibility anchor:** If the hypothetical isn't grounded in something real early, viewers dismiss it as fantasy and bounce.
- **Listing without guiding:** Reading off names with no reasoning, trade-offs, or POV turns it into a low-effort roll call.
- **Burying the hook:** Starting with setup or context instead of the provocative claim. The impossible-sounding premise must come first.
- **Over-long construction:** Too many components dilutes momentum. Cut to the 4–6 choices that carry an argument.
- **No emotional register:** Delivering speculation flatly. Passion, mild outrage, or genuine excitement is what makes the take feel alive.
- **CTA that doesn't loop:** Missing the "which next?" hook means no engagement-driving comment bait and no series momentum.

---

## 6. Abstracted Structural Templates

### Template A — "The Defiant Counterfactual"
> **Architecture:** Open by claiming an outcome that conventional wisdom says is impossible, delivered straight to camera with a "hear me out." → Drop a historical parallel where the same impossible thing already happened. → Walk through the mechanism that made it work then, mapping each element onto the present situation. → Land on a confident verdict: it's not crazy, it's a pattern. → CTA that dares the viewer to remember this prediction.

*Best for: crisis moments, sackings, "they're finished" narratives.*

---

### Template B — "The Constructed Alternate Reality"
> **Architecture:** Pose a structural "what if" (merged league, redrawn competition). → Anchor it instantly in a real proposal or real-world precedent from another sport/context. → Build the entity piece by piece, justifying each inclusion with size/history/strength reasoning and a clear personal stance on the tough calls. → Reveal a single quantified payoff (combined value, ranking) that proves the concept's scale. → Argue the emotional upside (rivalries, title races, viewership). → Close with "would this beat [the benchmark]?" → CTA inviting the next build.

*Best for: geographic thought experiments, format reinventions, league-building series.*

---

### Template C — "The Consequence Cascade"
> **Architecture:** Open with a conditional that triggers a chain of consequences ("If X happens, then what?"). → Establish the real stakes with current data (form, table, fixtures). → Trace the cascade logically — first effect, then the hidden second effect most people miss. → Apply it to a specific recognisable subject to make it concrete. → Deliver a verdict on who's most affected or what's most likely. → CTA: "follow to see how this plays out."

*Best for: relegation/wages, contract clauses, financial fallout, transfer-window squad-building scenarios.*

---

*Golden rule across all three: the hypothetical only works if the presenter treats it with total conviction. The audience forgives any premise — they don't forgive a creator who doesn't believe their own take.*
