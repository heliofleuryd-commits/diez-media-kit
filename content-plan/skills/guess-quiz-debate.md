# Format Skill File: Interactive Quiz / Picking-Sides Debate

## Format DNA
A two-person (or presenter-to-camera) format built around **forced binary choices** that escalate in difficulty. The viral engine is the tension between *honesty* and *bias* — a fan is repeatedly asked to choose, and the drama lives in whether they'll betray their own loyalty for the truth. Every answer is a mini hot take.

---

## 1. Structural Pattern

**Hook → Stakes Setup → Escalating Choices → Climax Choice → Verdict → CTA**

- **Hook:** A single bold question that frames the entire premise and demands a POV ("Who actually has X?"). Often loaded with an honesty challenge ("Be honest.").
- **Stakes Setup:** Establish the *rules of bias* — the responder has a known allegiance, which makes their answers carry weight. This is the secret sauce: we know who they "should" pick.
- **Escalating Choices:** A ladder of binary match-ups, each harder than the last. Early picks are easy/obvious (build trust in their honesty), middle picks introduce friction, late picks force a betrayal of self-interest.
- **Climax Choice:** The final match-up always pits the responder's own team/bias against the "correct" answer. Maximum tension.
- **Verdict:** The payoff line — they either stay loyal (controversial) or betray themselves (respect-earning). Either way it's a quote-worthy moment.
- **CTA:** Hand the decision to the viewer ("You decide," "Comments, help me out") — invites the debate to continue in comments.

---

## 2. Pacing Template

| Time | Beat | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| **0–4s** | Hook question + honesty challenge | Frame the debate, establish POV stakes |
| **4–10s** | First easy pick | Prove the responder is "fair" — builds trust |
| **10–35s** | Escalating ladder of picks | Rhythm of choices; tension climbs each round |
| **35–48s** | Climax / self-betrayal pick | Emotional peak — bias vs. truth |
| **48–55s** | Verdict line | The quotable payoff |
| **55–60s** | CTA to viewer | Push to comments / "you decide" |

Keep it cinematic between picks — let a tough choice *breathe* with a beat of silence or a reaction shot before the answer lands.

---

## 3. Visual Rhythm

- **Cut frequency:** Medium-fast on the rapid picks (cut on each answer), but slow down deliberately for the climax — hold on the face during the hard decisions.
- **On-camera split:** ~50% two-shot / presenter-to-camera, ~50% b-roll of the players or clubs being debated. Each name dropped earns a half-second of relevant footage.
- **Text overlay:** Each binary choice rendered as on-screen text (Option A **vs** Option B). Highlight the chosen one in a colour as they answer. A running tally or "ladder" graphic can show momentum.
- **B-roll pattern:** Match-up names trigger short, high-quality clips (goal, fan tifo, celebration) — never generic. The b-roll should *sell* why a choice is hard.
- **Music:** Low-energy bed under picks, swelling toward the climax choice, then a drop or silence on the verdict.

---

## 4. When To Use This Format

- **Tier/loyalty debates:** best fans, best atmosphere, biggest club — anything with passionate tribal stakes.
- **Head-to-head nation or squad comparisons:** position-by-position "who's better" builds a natural ladder.
- **Prediction brackets:** tournament trees where each round is a forced pick.
- **"Name X players who…"** recall challenges that double as debate fodder.
- Best deployed around **derbies, international breaks, tournament draws, and rivalry weeks** when tribal emotion is already high.

The format thrives when the responder has a **known bias** the audience can hold them to.

---

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **No stakes / neutral responder:** if we don't know their allegiance, the "honesty" tension evaporates. The whole format dies without a POV to betray or defend.
- **Flat difficulty curve:** if every pick is equally easy, there's no climax. The ladder MUST escalate.
- **Burying the controversy:** the verdict needs to be a clear, quotable, divisive line. Mushy "it depends" endings kill shares.
- **Too fast, no breathing room:** rattling through picks at list-speed removes the drama. Let the hard ones land.
- **Lazy b-roll:** generic stadium shots that don't *justify* why a choice is agonising. Each clip must raise the stakes.
- **Weak CTA:** ending on the answer instead of throwing the debate to the viewer wastes the comment-war engine.
- **Even-handed coward energy:** the audience punishes fence-sitting. A wrong-but-bold take outperforms a safe one.

---

## 6. Abstracted Structural Templates

### Template A: The Loyalty Gauntlet
> **Architecture:** Establish a responder with a stated allegiance → present a ladder of binary "who's better/bigger" choices in their domain → each round forces them closer to choosing against their own side → final match-up is *their team vs. the rival* → verdict either confirms or defies their bias → hand the final ruling to the audience.
>
> **Engine:** The tension between tribal loyalty and intellectual honesty. We watch to see if they'll betray themselves.

### Template B: The Bracket Prophet
> **Architecture:** Open with a bold prediction premise framed as a challenge → walk through a tournament tree round by round → each round is a confident pick delivered with a one-line justification → reach the final via a chain of decisions → name a champion with a passionate closing argument → throw the final winner to the viewer.
>
> **Engine:** Confident foresight as a flex. Every pick is a stake in the ground viewers want to argue with.

### Template C: The Recall Showdown
> **Architecture:** Pose a knowledge challenge with a hard numeric target → responder names obvious answers first (build momentum) → difficulty ramps as obvious picks run out → hit a wall near the target → openly invite the audience to supply the missing answer → end on unresolved tension.
>
> **Engine:** Collaborative incompleteness — leaving the puzzle unfinished forces comments to "solve" it.

---

**Core principle for all three:** the responder must have a *spine*. Football virality in this format comes from a person willing to make a divisive call and own it — never from neutral fact-listing.
