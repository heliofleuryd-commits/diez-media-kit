# Format Skill File: Contrarian Hook + Hidden-Story Reveal

## Format DNA
This format weaponises a deliberate gap between **what everyone thinks happened** and **what actually happened**. The presenter positions themselves as the only person who sees the truth, then guides the viewer through a buried narrative that recontextualises a moment everyone witnessed but no one understood. The emotional core is almost always *misjudgement corrected* — a player, manager, or event the world got wrong.

---

## 1. Structural Pattern

The arc runs in five beats:

1. **Contrarian Hook** — A bold claim that contradicts the obvious read of an event. Frames the presenter as holding secret knowledge ("No one understands…", "Everyone's making the same mistake…", "This isn't a good thing…"). It picks a fight with the consensus.

2. **The Misdirection** — Names the surface-level story everyone is *actually* talking about ("While everyone talks about X…") and explicitly sets it aside. This signals the real story is hidden and raises the stakes of the reveal.

3. **The Buried Setup** — Rewinds in time. Drops to a vulnerable origin moment — rejection, illness, family tragedy, a childhood verdict. Builds emotional investment in a single human. This is the longest, slowest section.

4. **The Recontextualising Reveal** — Connects the buried backstory to the present moment, making the opening event suddenly mean something completely different. The "goal" becomes a tribute; the "arrogance" becomes shyness; the "celebration" becomes a message. The payoff resolves the hook's tension.

5. **Open-Loop CTA** — Hands the verdict to the audience with a divisive either/or question ("Is he the best, or something we've never seen?" / "So was it deserved?"). Invites the comment-section war.

---

## 2. Pacing Template (≈45–75s)

| Time | Beat | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| **0–4s** | Contrarian hook to camera | Stop the scroll with a claim that feels *wrong* until explained |
| **4–10s** | The misdirection | Acknowledge the obvious story, dismiss it, tease the hidden one |
| **10–18s** | The pivot | "But the real reason is…" — point the viewer backward in time |
| **18–45s** | Buried setup (emotional core) | Slow, cinematic. Reveal the origin wound. Let it breathe |
| **45–58s** | Recontextualising reveal | Snap the past onto the present — the moment now means something else |
| **58–70s** | Resolution line | One clean, emotionally loaded summary sentence |
| **70–75s** | Open-loop CTA | Divisive question handed to the audience |

**Rhythm note:** The hook is fast and punchy; the setup deliberately slows down (silence, music swell); the reveal accelerates again. The tempo curve is *fast → slow → fast*, never flat.

---

## 3. Visual Rhythm

- **On-camera ratio:** Presenter delivers the hook, the pivot ("but here's what no one gets"), and the CTA straight to lens. B-roll/match footage carries the setup and reveal. Roughly 40–50% face time.
- **Cut frequency:** Slower than typical TikTok — 2–4s per clip during setup, tightening to 1–2s during the reveal acceleration. Cuts land on emotional or narrative beats, not arbitrarily.
- **B-roll discipline:** Every clip illustrates the *exact* line being spoken — the rejection, the trophy, the celebration, the injury. No filler. Footage earns its place by advancing the narrative.
- **Text overlay:** Sparse. Used to punch the hook claim, name a key figure, or stamp a number/stat. Never wall-to-wall captions.
- **Music:** Single emotional track that swells under the buried setup and peaks at the reveal. Intentional use of near-silence before the biggest beat.
- **The visual "snap":** At the reveal, cut back to the opening moment (the goal, the celebration) now that the viewer knows what it means — visually closing the loop.

---

## 4. When To Use This Format

Strongest fits:
- **A widely-seen moment with an untold backstory** — a celebration, a goal, a snub, a missed call that everyone reacted to but few understood.
- **A misjudged personality** — a player labelled arrogant/lazy/selfish who is actually shy/grieving/loyal.
- **Tragedy or sacrifice behind a triumph** — illness, loss, family, a promise kept.
- **A manager or player the football world mocked** who is now being vindicated.
- **A "rivalry-in-disguise"** — two teammates or stars on a collision course beneath a surface success.
- **A hidden mechanic / rules quirk** (trophy ownership, ball tech, squad rules) that overturns an assumption — the rare "fact" version, but still framed contrarian.

Avoid for: pure tactical breakdowns, neutral news updates, or moments with no emotional or counterintuitive angle.

---

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Hook that doesn't actually contradict consensus** — if the claim feels obvious, there's no tension to resolve and no reason to stay.
- **Burying the lead too long** — if the setup drags without re-teasing the reveal, viewers bounce before the payoff. Keep micro-hooks alive ("but that's not even why this matters").
- **Reveal that under-delivers** — the secret story must justify the bold hook. A weak payoff feels like clickbait and kills trust + saves/shares.
- **Flat tempo** — if pacing stays uniform, the cinematic *slow-down → snap* effect is lost and it reads as a generic list.
- **Over-explaining the CTA** — a vague or non-divisive closing question gets no comments. The verdict must be genuinely contestable.
- **Footage that doesn't match the words** — generic b-roll breaks the "every clip is evidence" spell and feels low-effort.
- **No clear POV** — the presenter must *believe* the contrarian take. Neutral narration kills the passion this format runs on.

## 6. Abstracted Structural Templates

### Template A — "The Misjudged Human"
> **Architecture:** Open by attacking the consensus read of a person ("Everyone's wrong about X for the same reason"). Dismiss the surface trait the crowd sees. Rewind to a childhood verdict or rejection that explains the *real* trait. Reveal that the misread quality is actually something sympathetic. Snap forward to a present stat or moment that proves it. CTA: ask the audience to re-judge them.

*Best for: players labelled arrogant, lazy, selfish, or disrespectful.*

### Template B — "The Hidden Tribute / Sacrifice"
> **Architecture:** Open by calling a celebrated moment "a problem" or framing it as misunderstood. Acknowledge the obvious storyline, set it aside. Reveal a private tragedy or sacrifice (loss, illness, a promise) that the moment was secretly *for*. Reconnect: the goal/win/return was an act of love or defiance, not a sporting event. Close on a single emotional resolution line, then a soft CTA.

*Best for: emotional moments — tribute goals, comebacks, returns from injury, World Cup squad stories.*

### Template C — "The Buried Mechanic / Quiet Truth"
> **Architecture:** Open with a claim that feels factually impossible or unfair ("If they win this, they won't actually win it"). Walk the viewer through a hidden rule, technology, or institutional decision they never noticed. Build small surprise reveals along the way. Land on the unfairness or strangeness of the truth. CTA: ask whether it's fair / fake / a problem — forcing a stance.

*Best for: rules quirks, trophy lore, tech in the game, "is this rigged/fake" hooks.*

---

**Core principle across all three:** the viewer must *think they understand* the opening image, then be shown they never did. The reveal is not new information — it's a new *meaning* for something they already saw.
