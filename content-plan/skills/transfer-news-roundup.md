# Format Skill File: Transfer/Club News Breakdown & Roundup

## Overview
This format aggregates multiple club news items (transfers, injuries, lineups, managerial moves) into a single guided narrative. The strongest versions don't just *list* news — they filter every item through a confident POV, turning a roundup into an argument. The presenter is a trusted insider who tells you what to *feel* about each development, not just what happened.

---

## 1. Structural Pattern

**The Arc: Hook → Agenda → Item Stack → Verdict → CTA**

- **Hook** — A bold "breaking" claim or a provocative framing question delivered straight to camera. Establishes stakes and POV in one breath.
- **Agenda Tease** *(roundup variant)* — A rapid preview of the 2–3 stories coming, creating a "stay till the end" contract.
- **Item Stack** — Each news beat follows a micro-arc: *fact → source citation → personal verdict*. The verdict is what separates this from neutral news. The presenter never reports without reacting.
- **Connective Tissue** — Items are linked with transitional momentum ("but here's where it gets interesting," "and that's not all") so the video feels like one story, not a checklist.
- **Verdict / Emotional Payoff** — A final synthesis: what this all *means* for the club, often with a hot take or future projection.
- **CTA** — A question stack inviting debate + follow prompt. The question is always opinion-baiting, never factual.

**The single-story variant** (lineup predictions, one transfer breakdown) drops the stack and goes deeper on one item: hook → context → POV-driven walkthrough → strong declarative conclusion → CTA.

---

## 2. Pacing Template (≈60–90s)

| Time | Beat | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| 0–4s | **Hook** | Bold claim or question to camera. Name the club, name the stakes. |
| 4–10s | **Agenda tease** | "We've got a lot to cover" — preview the items. *(roundup only)* |
| 10–28s | **Item 1** | Fact → source → verdict. Strongest/most controversial story first. |
| 28–46s | **Item 2** | Same micro-arc. Use a momentum connector to enter. |
| 46–62s | **Item 3** | Same micro-arc. Often the "and finally" softer/forward-looking beat. |
| 62–75s | **Verdict** | Synthesis + hot take on what it all means. |
| 75–85s | **CTA** | Opinion-baiting question stack + follow prompt. |

**Single-story version:** stretch Item 1 across 10–55s with deeper POV, then verdict + CTA.

> **Key principle:** lead with the most divisive item. The first verdict should make a portion of viewers want to argue immediately — that drives the comment engagement the CTA later harvests.

---

## 3. Visual Rhythm

- **On-camera / b-roll split:** ~50/50. Presenter delivers hooks, verdicts, and transitions to camera; b-roll covers the factual beats (player footage, training clips, badge graphics).
- **Cut frequency:** Medium — every 3–6s. Cuts land on verdict moments and emotional beats, *not* mechanically. Let a strong take breathe before cutting.
- **Text overlay:** Player names, transfer fees, and source outlets pinned as on-screen captions during factual beats. Keep verdicts spoken (not captioned) so the POV feels personal.
- **B-roll discipline:** Every clip earns its place — a player's goal when discussing their value, training footage for a fitness story. Avoid generic stock filler.
- **Audio:** Subtle bed under factual beats; let it drop or shift on the hook and final verdict to mark emotional gear changes.
- **Visual signature:** A consistent intro framing (presenter, same setup) builds the "daily show" recognition that powers return viewers in the roundup variant.

---

## 4. When To Use This Format

**Best suited for:**
- Periods of high club activity — transfer windows, managerial changes, pre-match buildup.
- Clubs with massive, opinionated fanbases (Real Madrid, Barcelona) where every micro-update has emotional stakes.
- Injury/availability news ahead of a big fixture.
- Lineup-prediction content before must-win matches (single-story variant).
- Building a recurring "daily" series for a specific club — the format's repeatability is its strength.

**Ideal trigger moments:**
- A cluster of 3+ small stories that individually aren't worth a video but stack into one.
- A single "here we go" confirmed signing that warrants a deeper POV breakdown.

---

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Neutral narration.** Reading news without a verdict kills it. If you don't tell viewers how to feel, you're a wire service, not a creator.
- **No hierarchy.** Treating all three items as equal flattens the arc. Lead strong, escalate, land.
- **Weak hook.** "Here's the latest news" promises nothing. The hook must threaten, excite, or provoke.
- **Source dump without stance.** Listing journalists adds credibility but becomes filler if not followed by *your* read on it.
- **Factual CTA.** "What do you think will happen?" is weak. Bait an *opinion* divide instead.
- **B-roll mismatch.** Generic footage that doesn't match the story breaks immersion and signals low effort.
- **Flat pacing.** Same energy throughout. The verdict beats need an audible/visual gear change.
- **Too many items.** Beyond 3–4 stories the viewer loses the thread and the arc collapses into a list.

## 6. Abstracted Structural Templates

### Template A — "The Confident Roundup"
```
HOOK: Bold framing claim naming the club + promise of essential updates (to camera)
TEASE: Rapid preview of 2–3 stories ("a lot to cover today")
ITEM 1 [most divisive]: fact → source → strong personal verdict
CONNECTOR: momentum bridge
ITEM 2: fact → source → verdict
CONNECTOR: "and finally..."
ITEM 3 [forward-looking]: fact → source → verdict
SYNTHESIS: what this all means for the club + hot take
CTA: stacked opinion-baiting questions + follow prompt
```
*Use when:* you have 3 distinct stories of varying weight. The recurring series workhorse.

---

### Template B — "The Single-Story Deep POV"
```
HOOK: Declarative claim about ONE move/development (to camera)
CONTEXT: why this matters now / the backstory
POV WALKTHROUGH: break the story into 2–3 angles, each filtered through your stance
ESCALATION: introduce the complication or counter-argument
VERDICT: strong, unambiguous conclusion — pick a side
CTA: invite the opposing view to argue ("agree or not?")
```
*Use when:* one transfer, signing, or decision is big enough to carry the whole video. Maximises POV intensity.

---

### Template C — "The Prediction / Stance Build"
```
HOOK: "This is how [club] SHOULD do X" — frame as a must-win necessity (to camera)
PREMISE: state the constraints (injuries, suspensions, stakes)
BUILD: walk through your choices position-by-position / decision-by-decision,
       defending each with conviction and dismissing alternatives
PEAK: the most controversial pick gets the most passionate defence
PAYOFF: declare why this approach wins
CTA: challenge viewers to disagree + signal of fandom ("comment X if you agree")
```
*Use when:* pre-match lineup debates or "how they should fix the squad" content. Built entirely on taking sides.

---

**Core takeaway:** This format lives or dies on *verdict density*. The news is the skeleton; your opinion on every beat is the muscle. Report nothing without reacting, lead with controversy, and let the most divisive take breathe before you cut.
