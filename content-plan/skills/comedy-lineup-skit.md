# Format Skill File: Comedy Commentary / Lineup Skit Overlay

## Format DNA

This format hijacks the cadence of real football broadcasting — the squad announcement or live commentary — and weaponises it for comedy. The humour lives entirely in the **delivery rhythm**: a straight, authoritative "official" voice reels off names or play-by-play, while the content underneath collapses into absurdity (puns, cultural stereotypes, fake players, escalating chaos). The contrast between **serious format and ridiculous payload** is the engine.

Two sub-variants appear in the sample:
- **Lineup variant** (@bertettimelu): announcer reads a "squad" where every name is a pun, celebrity, cartoon character or cultural reference tied to a country.
- **Live commentary variant** (@mckallaster): a regional/accented commentator narrates real footage with savage, biased, joke-laden play-by-play.

---

## 1. Structural Pattern (The Arc)

**Hook → Format Lock → Escalating Reveal Chain → Punchline Button → (Implicit) CTA**

- **Hook:** Immediate adoption of the "official" frame — "And here's the lineup for [X]…" or a loaded biased premise ("the greatest nation in the world *against* Brazil"). No preamble. The viewer is dropped mid-broadcast.
- **Format Lock:** First one or two "real-sounding" entries establish legitimacy so the absurdity has something to break. The audience must briefly believe the format is sincere.
- **Escalating Reveal Chain:** A rapid sequence of payoff beats, each one a self-contained micro-joke. The list/commentary builds — early jokes are mild (slight puns), later ones get more absurd, more taboo, or more chaotic. Each reveal earns its own beat.
- **Punchline Button:** A final, biggest-swing line that either tops everything ("impossible to beat this team"), breaks the fourth wall, or names the creator themselves as a joke.
- **CTA:** Almost always *implicit* — the comment-bait is built in. The format invites "do my country next," correction-comments ("that's not even from that country"), and reply-debate. Virality is driven by tagging and request-comments, not a spoken CTA.

---

## 2. Pacing Template (15–45s typical)

| Time | Beat | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| **0–2s** | Hook / format adoption | "Here's the lineup for [country]" or biased commentary premise — straight delivery |
| **2–5s** | First 1–2 entries play it semi-straight | Establishes the format as "real" |
| **5–25s** | Escalation chain | Rapid-fire reveals, each a beat, jokes intensifying in absurdity/edge |
| **25–35s** | Peak chaos | The most taboo / surreal / biggest-swing jokes land here |
| **35–42s** | Punchline button | Top-of-everything final line or self-reference |
| **42–45s** | Dead-stop / deadpan tag | Dry closing reaction ("yeah, makes sense") that resets tone and invites the comment |

**Key timing rule:** Never break the rhythm of the "official" voice. The comedy comes from *consistent* pacing — the joke never gets a wink. Pauses are used as deadpan punctuation, not for laughs.

---

## 3. Visual Rhythm

- **Cut frequency:** Low-to-medium. This format leans on **audio rhythm over visual cuts**. The voice carries the pace.
- **Overlay style:**
  - *Lineup variant:* A formation graphic (the real football pitch + position layout) with text names appearing in sync with the voice. Names pop on-beat — this is the core visual gag, letting viewers *read* the pun as they hear it.
  - *Commentary variant:* Real match footage / clips, often slightly degraded "broadcast" feel, with occasional replay-style slowdowns to sell the "let's see that again" beat.
- **B-roll pattern:** Every clip serves the joke being spoken. Replays are used to "analyse" an absurd moment. No filler footage.
- **Text discipline:** Names/key punchlines on screen; commentary jokes usually NOT subtitled fully — the accent and delivery are part of the gag.
- **Audio:** Often a low broadcast-ambience bed or stadium tone to reinforce the "this is a real broadcast" frame.

---

## 4. When To Use This Format

**Best suited for:**
- **National team / country-based content** — taps tribal identity, invites "do mine next."
- **Major tournament windows** (World Cup, Euros, Copa América) — peak emotional investment in nations.
- **Rivalry moments** — the biased-commentary variant thrives on derby/rivalry contempt.
- **Cultural-stereotype comedy** that's affectionate rather than mean — plays on national icons, foods, music, cartoons.
- **Real chaotic match footage** that's already funny (pitch invasions, wild tackles, comedy own-goals).

**Ideal trigger moments:** a country trends for any reason, a rivalry fixture is announced, a tournament draw drops, or a clip of footballing chaos surfaces.

---

## 5. Common Failure Modes

1. **Breaking the straight-face frame.** The moment the voice signals "this is a joke," the contrast engine dies. The delivery must stay 100% committed to the official register.
2. **Front-loading the best joke.** If the funniest reveal lands first, the escalation chain deflates. Build, don't blow your load early.
3. **No "real" anchor.** If *every* entry is absurd from word one, there's nothing to subvert. You need 1–2 legit-sounding beats to make the absurd ones hit.
4. **Inconsistent pacing.** Slowing down for jokes, rushing through setups, or uneven beats kills the broadcast-cadence illusion.
5. **Mean-spirited over playful.** Stereotype humour that punches down reads as cruel, not clever, and kills shareability. The affection must show.
6. **No comment hook.** If the format doesn't naturally invite "do my country" or correction-debate, you lose the engagement loop that drives this format's reach.
7. **Over-subtitling commentary.** Spelling out every joke in the live-commentary variant removes the rewatch reward and the delivery charm.

## 6. Abstracted Structural Templates

### Template A — "The Official Roster Subversion"
**Architecture:** Adopt the exact cadence of a formation/squad announcement. Open with a clean "Here's the lineup for [GROUP]." Lock credibility with one straight entry. Then walk through positions in real broadcast order, where each "player" is a pun, icon, or reference tied to the group's identity — jokes escalating from mild to surreal. Insert a deadpan "wait, that one's not even from here" self-correction mid-list as a rhythm break. Close on a self-referential or impossible-to-top final name, then a dry one-line verdict. Visual: live formation graphic, names appearing on-beat.

> *POV demand:* The creator must have an opinion baked into the choices — which references are flattering vs. savage *is* the take.

---

### Template B — "The Biased Commentator"
**Architecture:** Narrate real footage as a live commentator with an openly partisan stance — one side is heroic, the other is contemptible, stated in the opening line. Maintain authentic broadcast pacing and accent. Layer running insults at the "enemy" side and absurd praise for "your" side. Use replay beats ("let's see that again") to milk a single chaotic moment for a second joke. Build to a result-confirmation finale that frames your side's win/loss as cosmic justice. End on a smug or self-deprecating button.

> *POV demand:* Tribal loyalty is the whole thesis — neutrality is forbidden. The commentator *is* a fan with a grudge.

---

### Template C — "The Chaos Replay Breakdown"
**Architecture:** Open on a genuinely absurd footage moment with a pseudo-analytical commentator frame. State authoritatively what "just happened." Cut to a slow-mo replay and "analyse" the absurdity in deadpan technical language (treating clowning as elite skill, or a foul as choreography). Escalate the analysis into increasingly ridiculous expert commentary. Button with a dry verdict, fake stat, or score/judgment ("ten points, perfectly executed").

> *POV demand:* The comedy is in the confident *misreading* — the creator's authoritative wrong-take is the personality.

---

**Format north star:** A deadly serious broadcast voice delivering completely unserious content, with a point of view smuggled inside every joke. The cadence is the costume; the take is the payload.
