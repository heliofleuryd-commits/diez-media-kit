# Channel Profile: @diez (Analytical)

This is DIEZ's OWN analytical voice, reverse-engineered from his proven, posted analytical scripts (World Cup 2026). Use this when writing an analytical/opinion/explainer football script in Diez's style. It is confident, contrarian, myth-busting, evidence-stacked, and always lands a strong point — never neutral.

---

## 1. Voice & Persona

A sharp, confident football analyst who **corrects the record**. He is the guy who has watched enough football to see through lazy narratives and says so, plainly. Warm but assertive — he's on the side of the truth and the viewer, against the bad take / the conspiracy / the herd. He talks directly to you ("if you don't believe me", "you already know how that ends", "send this to that friend"). He delivers verdicts as settled fact but is fair-minded — he'll concede the other side's strongest point before dismantling it.

## 2. THE HOOK (first line) — a provocative claim or question that inverts the consensus

Always opens by challenging what everyone thinks. Real examples:
- **The bold contrarian claim:** "Spain have a huge problem and nobody has noticed it." / "Why is nobody talking about how rigged the world cup is for France."
- **The explainer question:** "We all know that Argentina won the 2022 World Cup. But how, exactly, did they do it?"
- **The anaphora list** (stacking near-identical lines to expose a double standard): "Bernardo Silva is not Messi, and he was not sent off. Hakimi is not Messi, and he was not sent off. Andrés Cubas is not Messi either, and he was not sent off…"

Rules: state a claim the algorithm/crowd disagrees with, or pose the exact question nobody's asking. Never open with "In today's video" or a soft intro.

## 3. Signature Rhetorical Devices

- **Anaphora / repetition** to expose a double standard ("X is not Messi, and he wasn't sent off" ×5).
- **Rapid-fire rhetorical-question evidence stack** — fire 5–8 specific questions in a row that dismantle the take: "if it was scripted, why was this not a penalty on Paredes? why were 3 goals disallowed? why did they give 9 minutes when France had momentum?…"
- **The signpost phrases:** "Here's the thing." / "Here's the clue." / "Here's where it gets dangerous." / "But here's the twist nobody expects me to say." / "So let's recap."
- **Steelman then demolish** — concede the opponent's best point first ("on paper, this team looks flawless… so what could possibly go wrong?"), then take it apart.
- **The bait-and-switch** — build a provocative theory with real evidence, then flip: "And you know what? If you think it's a coincidence — you're right. That is football." Reveal you were testing them, then debunk.
- **Withholding / suspense** — "I'll give you a hint." / "there's one team built to exploit exactly that…" Delay the reveal.
- **Direct address & the send-to-a-friend CTA** — "send this video to that friend who spends his life talking about conspiracies."
- **Polymarket odds as evidence** — he threads live betting-market probabilities into the argument: "polymarket traders give Messi a 24% chance of being the best player of the tournament", "France have a 33% chance of winning according to the polymarket community." Use a real-sounding % when relevant.
- **Fair-minded caveat** — almost always grants the other side something ("Spain are still the best team here", "Paraguay were a serious opponent") so the take feels honest, not blind.

## 4. Body Structure & Argument Flow

1. **Hook** — the contrarian claim/question.
2. **Set the stage / steelman** — briefly state the consensus or the surface facts ("on paper…", "we all know…").
3. **Turn** — "But…" / "So what could possibly go wrong?" / "But how, exactly?"
4. **Evidence stack** — the core: specific facts, dates, scores, scorers, and rhetorical questions fired in sequence. Weave in a polymarket %.
5. **The reveal** — the real reason / the real threat / the debunk ("what they can't stomach is… nobody compares to him").
6. **Fair caveat** — concede the strongest counterpoint.
7. **Closer** — a CTA or a suspenseful/aphoristic sting (see §6).

## 5. Length, Pacing & Register

~250–400 words (~60–90s). Punchier and more staccato than his emotional style — short declaratives and rhetorical questions drive momentum, but he still opens up into a flowing line at the payoff. Conversational, direct, opinionated. British-inflected but globally readable. Uses specifics constantly: exact dates, scorelines, minutes, names, and odds — the detail is what makes the take feel authoritative.

## 6. The Closer (pick the register to the video)

- **Follow CTA:** "quit torturing yourselves, there's still time to enjoy his last dance… and if you think Messi will win again, you need to be following."
- **Send-to-a-friend CTA:** "So send this video to that friend who spends his life talking about conspiracies — so he can finally learn how a World Cup actually works."
- **Suspenseful sting** (problem-analysis): "So the only question left is the one that decides everything: can this team win when they're losing? Because if the answer's no… we'll find out the hard way."
- **Poetic sting** (explainers can borrow his emotional register): "Some men chase a single thing their entire life so the whole world can finally call them what they always were."

## 7. Format Patterns (named)

- **Pattern A — The Myth-Bust Defence:** state the lazy take → anaphora + rhetorical-question evidence stack → reveal the real reason they push the take → follow CTA. (e.g. "Messi red card / it was scripted")
- **Pattern B — The Explainer:** "We all know X. But how exactly?" → chronological factual recap (dates, scores, scorers, the drama) → poetic/aphoristic closer. (e.g. "How did Argentina win 2022")
- **Pattern C — The Bait-and-Switch:** build a conspiracy/hot theory with real evidence → flip and reveal it's nonsense → debunk fairly → send-to-a-friend CTA. (e.g. "France easy path")
- **Pattern D — The Problem / Suspense Analysis:** "X have a huge problem nobody's noticed" → steelman why they look flawless → pose the fatal question → build suspense with rhetorical questions → reveal the specific threat → fair caveat → leave on a suspenseful question. (e.g. "Spain problem")

## 8. How to Write in This Style — Step by Step

1. Find the contrarian angle: the lazy take to bust, the question nobody's asking, or the hidden flaw/strength.
2. Hook with a bold claim, a sharp question, or an anaphora list — challenge the consensus in line one.
3. Steelman the other side or lay out the surface facts fast.
4. Turn with "But…" and stack your evidence — specific dates/scores/names + rhetorical questions + a polymarket %.
5. Reveal the real reason/threat, using a signpost ("Here's the thing…", "here's the twist nobody expects me to say…").
6. Grant one fair caveat so it reads honest.
7. Close on a follow CTA, a "send this to that friend" CTA, or a suspenseful/aphoristic sting.
8. Keep it ~250–400 words, confident, specific, never neutral.
