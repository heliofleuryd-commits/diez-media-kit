# Format Skill File: Passionate Opinion / On-Camera Hot Take

## Format DNA
The presenter delivers a strongly-held verdict straight to camera and then *defends it like a lawyer*. This is not analysis — it's advocacy. The viewer is recruited into the presenter's emotional position within the first sentence, then carried through a chain of justifications that escalate conviction rather than soften it.

---

## 1. Structural Pattern (The Arc)

**Hook (Verdict Declaration)** → **Setup (Stakes / Why It Matters)** → **Evidence Cascade (escalating justifications)** → **Emotional Peak (the line that crystallises the take)** → **CTA (invite the fight)**

The defining feature: the *conclusion comes first*. Unlike narrative formats that build to a reveal, this format front-loads the opinion and uses the body to retroactively justify it. The "payoff" is not new information — it's the moment the presenter reaches maximum conviction and dares the audience to disagree.

Key beats:
- **Open with the verdict already decided.** No throat-clearing, no "let's discuss."
- **Name the villain or hero early.** A single person, decision, or narrative becomes the focal point.
- **Stack evidence in rising intensity.** Each point should feel angrier or more certain than the last.
- **Concede something briefly** (to appear credible) then immediately overrule it — "I'll admit X, but…"
- **Land an emotional peak line** — the quotable thesis that fans will repeat in comments.
- **Close by handing the mic to the audience** — explicitly inviting them to argue back.

---

## 2. Pacing Template (45–75s)

| Time | Beat | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| **0–4s** | Verdict hook delivered straight to camera | State the take as fact, not opinion. Name the subject. |
| **4–12s** | Stakes / framing | Why this matters now. Set the emotional temperature. |
| **12–35s** | Evidence cascade (3–4 points) | Each justification escalates conviction. Use rhetorical questions. |
| **35–50s** | The concession-then-override | Acknowledge a counterpoint, then crush it. Builds credibility. |
| **50–62s** | Emotional peak / thesis line | The most quotable, most extreme statement. Let it breathe. |
| **62–75s** | CTA — invite disagreement | "What do you think?" framed as a challenge, not a request. |

For the cinematic variant (@pechefootball / @5.at.the.back style), stretch the evidence cascade and use silence + music before the peak line.

---

## 3. Visual Rhythm

**Cut frequency:**
- On-camera segments: hold longer (4–8s per shot) to let conviction register on the face.
- During the evidence cascade: cut to b-roll/match footage *on each new point* — every clip illustrates the claim being made.
- Speed up cuts subtly as intensity rises toward the peak line.

**On-camera / b-roll split (~50/50):**
- Hook and CTA: always on-camera, eye contact with lens.
- Evidence cascade: b-roll of the moment being criticised/praised, presenter VO continues.
- Peak line: cut back to face. The thesis must be delivered to camera.

**Text overlay:**
- Minimal, intentional. Overlay only the verdict hook and the peak line as on-screen text.
- Avoid karaoke captions across the whole video — it cheapens the cinematic feel.
- Names/stats can flash briefly during the cascade if they sharpen the argument.

**B-roll discipline:**
- Every clip must *prove a point being made in that exact sentence.* No generic stadium filler.
- Use the specific moment (the substitution, the defensive lapse, the goal) as visual evidence.

---

## 4. When to Use This Format

**Best suited for:**
- **Post-match reactions** where one decision/person decided the outcome (manager tactics, a substitution, a defensive error).
- **Player legacy debates** — "is X the best/most underrated/most disrespected."
- **Squad selection controversies** — who should/shouldn't be picked.
- **Narrative pushback** — defending a player against media/fan consensus ("everyone's wrong about…").
- **Bold predictions** stated as near-certainty (title favourites, who'll bottle it).
- **Rivalry / fan-culture friction** — taking a tribal side openly.

**Trigger moments:**
- Immediately after a controversial result while emotion is high.
- When a dominant media narrative exists to push against (the most fertile ground — disagreement *with the crowd* generates the most comments).

---

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Neutral delivery.** The format dies the moment you sound balanced. No "on the other hand" without a knockout override.
- **Burying the verdict.** If the take isn't in the first sentence, viewers swipe before the payoff.
- **Listing instead of arguing.** Stacking facts without rising emotional intensity flattens it into a "did you know" video.
- **Weak villain/hero focus.** Diffuse opinions ("the whole team was bad") are forgettable. One target = one strong reaction.
- **Conceding too much.** A single brief concession adds credibility; multiple concessions destroy conviction.
- **A soft CTA.** "Like and subscribe" kills it. The CTA must provoke — demand they disagree.
- **B-roll that doesn't earn its place.** Generic footage breaks the lawyer-presenting-evidence illusion.
- **No quotable peak line.** Without one extreme, repeatable statement, the algorithm gets no comment fuel.
- **Hedging the prediction.** "Maybe they could possibly…" — commit fully or don't post.

## 6. Three Abstracted Structural Templates

### Template A — "The Single Cause Verdict" (post-match blame/credit)
```
HOOK:      Declare that one person/decision is solely responsible 
           for the result. State it as undeniable.
SETUP:     Frame why this is unforgivable / remarkable given the stakes.
CASCADE:   Walk through 3–4 specific decisions, each one building 
           your case. Use rhetorical questions to make the audience 
           agree before they realise it.
OVERRIDE:  "I wanted them to succeed / I'll give credit where due, 
           BUT…" — then double down.
PEAK:      Restate the verdict with maximum emotion — the line 
           that summarises your entire case.
CTA:       Throw it open — "tell me I'm wrong."
```

### Template B — "The Disrespect Defence" (narrative pushback for a player)
```
HOOK:      Call out the consensus opinion as offensive / disgraceful.
SETUP:     Identify the "culture" or "media" creating the narrative — 
           name the enemy.
CASCADE:   Recite the subject's overlooked qualities / adversity / 
           track record, escalating in admiration.
OVERRIDE:  Address the strongest criticism head-on, then dismantle it.
PEAK:      A definitive status claim — "he's a generational talent," 
           stated as beyond debate.
CTA:       Frame agreement as obvious; dare doubters to respond.
```

### Template C — "The Confident Prediction" (bold forecast as fact)
```
HOOK:      Reframe an event as bigger than it appears 
           ("this wasn't just X, it was a warning").
SETUP:     Establish the evidence of dominance / inevitability.
CASCADE:   Build the case with specifics — depth, quality, momentum — 
           each point reinforcing the forecast.
OVERRIDE:  Acknowledge the competition / risk briefly, then 
           reassert superiority.
PEAK:      State the prediction as near-certainty, with a stake 
           ("it'd be a disgrace if they don't…").
CTA:       Ask the audience for their pick — knowing yours is "correct."
```

---

**Golden rule:** In this format the presenter is never a narrator — they are a *fan with a microphone and an argument to win.* Conviction is the content. The moment you hedge, you lose.
