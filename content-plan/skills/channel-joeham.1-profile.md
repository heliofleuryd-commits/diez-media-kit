# Channel Profile: @joeham.1

## 1. Voice & Persona

The creator operates as an **insider storyteller** — a narrator who positions himself as someone with access to the full context behind a dramatic football moment that the casual viewer missed. The persona is confident and authoritative, but channels that authority into unpacking *what really happened* rather than offering pure opinion. He functions like a sports documentarian compressed into a minute: calm under the chaos he's describing, building tension deliberately, and occasionally stepping in with a personal verdict to remind viewers there's a human point of view driving the narration. The stance is "let me walk you through the drama you didn't fully understand."

## 2. Vocabulary Register

- **Informal, conversational register** with a distinctly British/colloquial cadence (clipped phrasing, dropped formality, casual interjections).
- **Moderate-to-high football jargon** — assumes the viewer knows positions, VAR protocol, aggregate scoring, automatic promotion, penalty shootout mechanics — but explains the *rules-based* nuances (e.g. handball technicalities, referee protocols) for the moments that hinge on them.
- **Signature structural tics:** opens many videos with a framing verb-of-realisation ("See..."), uses present tense to narrate past events for immediacy, leans on short reactive fragments to mimic crowd/player emotion, and inserts brief first-person verdicts ("for me...") to assert a POV.
- **Sentence rhythm** alternates between very short staccato fragments and longer explanatory sentences, creating a push-pull pace.

## 3. Recurring Rhetorical Devices

- **The "problem" setup** — Establishes that a team/player "had a problem" early, framing the entire video as the unravelling of a conflict.
- **The lesson-statement hook** — Opens with a universal football truism ("This is why you should never...") that the story then illustrates.
- **Present-tense play-by-play** — Narrates action as if live, even though it has already happened, for dramatic immediacy.
- **The withheld twist** — Signals that a moment *looked* resolved ("they thought they had won it") before revealing the reversal.
- **Rule explanation as reveal** — Pauses the drama to explain an obscure law or protocol, positioning himself as the knowledgeable guide.
- **Rhetorical question close** — Ends on an open question that projects forward ("but can they win it back to back?") to invite engagement.
- **Quoted aftermath** — Drops in what a player/manager said *after the game* as a punchy capstone or emotional twist.
- **Personal verdict injection** — Breaks neutrality with a direct opinion to assert stance ("for me, I genuinely cannot think of...").

## 4. Typical Length & Pacing

Videos run roughly **45–75 seconds**, structured as a tight narrative arc:

- **0–5s — Hook:** Bold framing statement, truism, or a "see, [subject] had a problem" setup.
- **5–20s — Context build:** Backstory or pre-match tension that the casual viewer wouldn't know.
- **20–45s — Escalation:** Present-tense play-by-play of the key moments, often with a "but then" pivot and a withheld twist.
- **45–60s — Reveal/turn:** The reversal, the rule explanation, or the emotional gut-punch.
- **Final 5–10s — Resolution + forward question:** Post-match quote, personal verdict, or a cliffhanger question that opens debate.

Pacing is **escalating, not rushed** — context breathes, then the action accelerates, then it lands a single emotional or unresolved beat.

## 5. Primary Audience Hook

The dominant emotional lever is **injustice and unresolved drama** — the sense that something dramatic, controversial, or unfair just happened, and you're only now learning the *full* story. Secondary levers: **schadenfreude** (collapses, bottling, missed penalties) and **vindication** (the underdog or wronged party getting their moment). The creator consistently frames events around a victim, a villain, and a twist — making the viewer want to take a side and finish the story.

## 6. Signature Format Patterns

### Template A — "The Hidden Conflict"
Opens by declaring a subject "had a problem" or had been "robbed/cheated." Walks back to reveal pre-match or behind-the-scenes context the viewer didn't know, then layers grievances chronologically, and closes with an aftermath quote or unanswered question. *Built on the promise of insider context.*

### Template B — "The Cautionary Tale"
Opens with a universal football lesson ("This is why you should never..."). The body of the video is a single dramatic match sequence that *proves* the lesson — usually featuring a premature celebration, a reversal, or a moment of hubris punished. Closes on the consequence or a wry observation.

### Template C — "The Withheld Twist Recap"
Narrates a high-stakes moment in present tense as though it resolved one way ("they thought they had won it"), then deploys a "but VAR / but then" pivot to flip the outcome. Often pauses mid-narrative to explain a rule or protocol that justifies the twist, then lands on the emotional fallout. *Built on subverting the expected result.*

**Note for adaptation to your target creator style (@pechefootball / @fiagoball / @5.at.the.back):** This creator's strength is *narrative withholding and insider framing*, but his tone is faster and more reactive than the cinematic, presenter-led style you're targeting.
