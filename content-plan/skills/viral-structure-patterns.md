# Viral Structure Patterns

# Cross-Channel Structural Patterns: Top Football Virality

*Analysis of structural mechanics only. Two distinct format families emerge: short comedic "lineup" bits (~23-33s) and long-form storytelling/explainer pieces (~57-121s). Patterns below note where families diverge.*

---

## 1. Pacing Patterns (Time Distribution)

### Storytelling / Explainer Family (~55-120s)
| Phase | Share of Runtime | Function |
|-------|-----------------|----------|
| **Hook** | First 3-6s (~5-8%) | Bold claim or provocative question to camera |
| **Setup / Context** | Next 25-40% | Establishes stakes, background, why it matters |
| **Escalation** | Middle 40-50% | Layered detail, rising tension, complication stacks |
| **Reveal / Payoff** | ~70-85% mark | The "aha" or emotional climax lands |
| **CTA / Button** | Final 3-8% | Quick close, often a follow prompt or rhetorical question |

The defining rhythm: **setup is generous, never rushed.** These videos let the premise breathe before escalating. Tension is built through *additive* structure — each beat raises the stakes of the previous one.

### Comedic Lineup Family (~23-33s)
| Phase | Share of Runtime | Function |
|-------|-----------------|----------|
| **Frame** | First 2-3s | "And here's the lineup for [team]" — instant format recognition |
| **Rapid Reveal Loop** | ~90% of runtime | Joke-per-beat cadence, no pause between hits |
| **Button** | Final 2-3s | Punchline twist or absurd capper |

This family inverts the storytelling pace: **no setup, pure escalation.** The format itself is the hook.

---

## 2. Reveal Timing

- **Storytelling pieces delay the core payoff to the 70-85% mark.** The most valuable information or emotional resolution is deliberately withheld until late. Earlier time is spent making the audience *want* the answer.
- **The "but" pivot is structural, not incidental.** Long-form pieces consistently build toward a reversal — the expected outcome is set up, then subverted. The reveal lands *at* or *just after* this pivot.
- **Comedic pieces have continuous micro-reveals** — every line is a small payoff, with the biggest gag reserved for the final beat.
- **Mystery-driven explainers plant the question in the first 5 seconds and answer it last**, using the entire body as a tease toward resolution.

---

## 3. CTA Patterns

Three dominant closing styles:

1. **The soft follow-prompt** — a brief, low-pressure invitation to follow framed around the *content category* ("for more stories like this"). Never aggressive; positioned as a service.
2. **The open question** — ending on a "what do you think?" that hands the debate to the audience. This is the strongest engagement driver for opinion-led pieces.
3. **The narrative button (no CTA)** — comedic and emotional pieces frequently end on a punchline or poignant final line with *no* explicit ask. The content itself is the retention mechanism.

**Key structural insight:** The highest-view videos rarely lead with a hard CTA. The follow prompt, when present, is the *last* thing — never interrupting the narrative.

---

## 4. On-Screen Text Patterns

- **Text reinforces, never replaces, narration.** It anchors key names, numbers, and locations the viewer needs to hold in memory.
- **In data-heavy explainers**, text appears synchronised with each new fact to reduce cognitive load — the viewer reads and hears simultaneously.
- **In comedic pieces**, text often delivers the visual punchline (the absurd name appearing on screen as it's spoken) — a second comedic layer.
- **Hook text frequently mirrors the spoken opening line** to capture sound-off viewers in the first 2 seconds.
- Text density rises in the body, drops at the emotional/punchline moments — letting the climactic beat stand alone.

---

## 5. Sound / Music Patterns

- **Storytelling family uses music as an emotional dynamic.** Music swells under the setup, often drops to near-silence at the reveal to make the payoff land, then returns for the close.
- **Silence is a deliberate tool** — used right before a key reveal or after a heavy line to let it breathe (especially in the tribute/emotional format).
- **Comedic pieces run continuous, energetic backing** that maintains pace and signals "this is fun" from the first frame.
- **B-roll cuts are timed to vocal beats**, not the reverse — every visual change punctuates a spoken point rather than filling space.
- The pivot moment ("but...") is frequently marked by an *audio shift* — a beat drop, music change, or sudden quiet.

---

## 6. The Single Most Consistent Pattern

> **Every top performer opens with a bold, POV-loaded statement or provocative question delivered straight to the viewer — then withholds resolution until late, forcing the audience to stay for the payoff.**

Across both families, the structural DNA is identical:
1. **A confident opening claim that takes a side** (a stance, a mystery, a "this is the greatest/worst/strangest" framing).
2. **A deliberate gap** between the promise and its fulfilment.
3. **An escalating, additive middle** that keeps raising stakes rather than listing facts flatly.
4. **A reveal or punchline held to the final quarter.**

The neutral-narrator approach appears in **zero** of these videos. The presenter (or format) always has a clear, passionate point of view — the structure exists to deliver *that opinion* with maximum tension and payoff.

---

## Templates Worth Replicating (Structural Only)

**The "Bold Claim → Withheld Proof" arc** (storytelling)
`[Provocative stance to camera] → [why it shouldn't be possible] → [the escalating evidence] → [the late reveal] → [open question or soft follow]`

**The "Mystery Premise → Smart Answer" arc** (explainer)
`[Strange observation as question] → [debunk the obvious explanation] → [layered real reasoning] → [final twist] → "what do you think?"`

**The "Expected → Subverted" pivot** (debate/news)
`[Set up the assumed outcome] → [build false confidence] → ["but..."] → [the reversal] → [consequence]`

All three demand a POV in the opening 5 seconds and a payoff in the final 25%.
