# Hook Guide — How to Write & Choose Hooks

A practical, decision-based guide to writing football hooks. Not just what exists — but how to pick the right type for your topic, how to construct it, and what kills it before the viewer decides to stay.

---

## The One Rule That Overrides Everything

**The hook is not a description of your video. It is a provocation that makes watching feel mandatory.**

A neutral opening ("today we're looking at...") is not a hook — it's a forfeit. Every hook in this guide creates an emotional response within 3 seconds: outrage, curiosity, nostalgia, urgency, or the itch of disagreement. If your opening doesn't trigger one of those, rewrite it.

---

## Decision Map — Which Hook Type to Use

Use this first. Match your topic type to the right hook family.

| Topic Type | Best Hook Family | Second Option |
|---|---|---|
| Single match moment / incident | Shock | Controversy |
| Tactical breakdown / system | Curiosity ("secret process") | Contrarian |
| Player ranking / GOAT debate | Contrarian | Controversy |
| Transfer / upcoming event | FOMO | Shock |
| Forgotten player / underdog | Nostalgia | Curiosity |
| Why a team failed / collapsed | Controversy | Contrarian |
| Career tribute / retrospective | Nostalgia | Shock (stat that reframes them) |
| "The real reason" explainer | Contrarian | Curiosity |
| Stats / data-driven analysis | Curiosity | Shock |
| Series opener | FOMO ("Part 1 of...") | Curiosity |

## Hook Type 1 — Shock

**Core mechanic:** State an extreme fact, consequence, or label as settled truth before any context. The viewer's brain flags it as impossible and stays to understand how.

### How to write it step by step
1. Identify the most extreme true thing about your topic (a scoreline, a stat, a consequence, a decision).
2. State it first — no intro, no "today," no name-drop. Just the fact, flat.
3. Let it sit for one beat — don't immediately explain it.
4. Then begin the story that earns it.

### Templates
- `"[Team/player] was [extreme negative label]. That's not an opinion. Here's what happened."`
- `"[Extreme consequence] — because of one decision."`
- `"[Player] destroyed [season/tournament/career] in [number] minutes."`
- Consequence-first: `"Players were refusing to play. Staff were threatening to walk. Here's why."`

### What kills it
- Hedging: "some might say," "you could argue," "kind of." Commit or it dies.
- Over-qualifying the hook: if you explain it before delivering it, the shock evaporates.
- Stakes that don't match the setup — if you promise devastation, deliver devastation.

### Strongest topics
Single incidents, player meltdowns, managerial disasters, shock results, extreme stats.

## Hook Type 2 — Curiosity

**Core mechanic:** Open an information gap the viewer can't close without watching. They must know the answer — so they stay.

### How to write it step by step
1. Identify a specific, real thing in your video that sounds impossible or counterintuitive.
2. Name it in one sentence as a question or as a statement that implies one.
3. Do NOT answer it immediately — the gap must stay open for at least 15-20 seconds.
4. Build toward the answer; don't deliver it early.

### Templates
- `"When [player] does [observable thing], something happens that fans never notice."`
- `"[Team] had the worst [metric] in Europe. They still won the title. Here's how."`
- `"Nobody has ever explained why [specific thing happened]. Until now."`
- `"This is [player name]. You've never heard of him. You're about to wish you had."`
- `"[Club] spent [amount] on [player]. There's a reason nobody talks about what happened next."`

### What kills it
- A gap that's too vague or too small to matter ("did you know football is interesting?").
- Answering the gap in the hook itself — you've closed it before it opened.
- A "curious" setup with a boring payoff — the viewer feels cheated and won't come back.

### Strongest topics
Tactical systems, hidden details, emerging players, suppressed stories, "the real reason" formats.

## Hook Type 3 — Controversy

**Core mechanic:** Assign blame, pick a villain, or take a side that splits the room. The viewer either agrees furiously or disagrees furiously — either way they stay.

### How to write it step by step
1. Name a person, decision, or event.
2. Make a single, strong accusation or judgment — no balance, no both-sides.
3. The claim must be defensible with real evidence you'll deliver in the video.
4. The audience must feel your conviction — flat delivery kills controversy.

### Templates
- `"[Name] cost [team] [trophy/season]. Alone. Let me show you."`
- `"What happened in [match/event] was not football. It was [extreme noun]."`
- `"[Player/manager] should not be allowed to [do X] anymore."`
- `"I hated what [player] did [in this match/moment]. And so did [everyone watching]."`
- `"[Tournament/institution] is corrupt. The evidence has been there for years."`

### What kills it
- Fake balance — "but you could argue both sides." You cannot. Pick one.
- Controversy without evidence — this is just noise, not argument.
- Going after something trivial — the stakes must feel real.

### Strongest topics
VAR/refereeing, managerial mistakes, player attitudes, institutional corruption, big-club bias.

## Hook Type 4 — Nostalgia

**Core mechanic:** Reach into the past to create a specific felt emotion — loss, reverence, "what could have been." The viewer recognises something they'd almost forgotten and stays to experience it again.

### How to write it step by step
1. Name the era, player, or moment specifically — vague nostalgia doesn't land.
2. Deliver the emotion in the opening line — don't build to it; start in it.
3. Give the viewer something they'd forgotten: a specific stat, a moment, a detail that reframes the whole memory.
4. Let it breathe. This is the one hook type where pace > urgency.

### Templates
- `"Sometimes we forget what [player] actually was."`
- `"In [year], [player/team] did something that will never happen again."`
- `"When [player] was [young/injured/released] — before everything — [specific thing was true]."`
- `"[Player] predicted this [years ago]. He was right about everything."`
- `"There was a version of [player's] career where he becomes the greatest of all time. This is it."`

### What kills it
- Rushing it — nostalgia needs space.
- Treating a legend's career as a list of stats rather than a felt experience.
- Over-explaining — let the moments speak.

### Strongest topics
Retired legends, career retrospectives, historic matches, tributes, "what could have been" arcs.

## Hook Type 5 — FOMO

**Core mechanic:** Signal that something big is happening now or imminently — and the viewer who doesn't watch will be left behind.

### How to write it step by step
1. Name the event, countdown, or deadline with specificity — real numbers outperform vague urgency.
2. Imply motion — use language of momentum ("just happened," "it's finally here," "right now").
3. Make clear what the viewer stands to miss if they scroll past.

### Templates
- `"In [X days], everything about [team/league] changes. Here's what nobody's saying."`
- `"[Transfer/event] is happening. Here's what it actually means."`
- `"Before you [buy this/watch this/believe this] — watch this first."`
- `"This is the start of [series name]. Everything connects from here."`
- `"[Event] hasn't happened in [X] years. It's about to."`

### What kills it
- Manufactured urgency around something trivial — the viewer feels played.
- Vague FOMO ("something big is coming") without a real hook underneath.
- Overusing "breaking" or "huge" until they become wallpaper.

### Strongest topics
Transfers, tournament previews, product releases, series content, once-in-a-generation moments.

---

## Hook Type 6 — Contrarian

**Core mechanic:** Name a belief the viewer genuinely holds — then immediately challenge it with confidence. They stay because they want to prove you wrong, or because you've shaken something they thought was settled.

### How to write it step by step
1. Name the consensus belief clearly and fairly — the viewer must recognise it as their own.
2. Reject it directly ("But that's wrong. Here's why.") — don't soften.
3. Deliver a credible, specific alternative argument. The "not X, not Y — it's Z" rhythm is the most effective structure.
4. Build to the proof in the body — the hook is the challenge, the video is the case.

### Templates
- `"If I told you [consensus belief], I'd be lying to you."`
- `"[Popular take] — everyone believes it. Everyone is wrong."`
- `"It's not [obvious explanation]. It's not [second obvious explanation]. It's [real answer]."`
- `"[Team/player] has a problem. Nobody wants to talk about it."`
- `"[Player] is not the best [X] in the world. Here's the player nobody watches who actually is."`

### What kills it
- A "contrarian" take that's actually the mainstream view — it reads as hollow.
- Backing down in the payoff — if you can't make the case, don't open with the challenge.
- Edginess without an argument — the video must earn the hook.

### Strongest topics
Player rankings, GOAT debates, tactical hot takes, undervalued players, "the real reason" formats.

---

## The "Not X — It's Z" Device

This single structure appears across Shock, Curiosity, and Contrarian hooks and is the most reusable device in the entire library:

`"It's not [the first thing everyone thinks]. It's not [the second thing]. It's [the actual answer]."`

It works because it:
1. Pre-empties the viewer's first two guesses (making them feel smart for having thought of them)
2. Creates tension as both guesses are rejected
3. Forces the reveal into the final slot — giving it maximum weight

Use it whenever your video's actual answer is more surprising than the obvious explanations.

## Combination Hooks

The top-performing hooks often stack two types. The most effective combinations:

| Stack | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Shock + Curiosity** | "Something extreme happened — and there's a hidden reason for it." |
| **Contrarian + Curiosity** | "You're wrong about this — and the real answer is stranger than you think." |
| **Nostalgia + Shock** | "You remember this player fondly — but here's a fact that reframes everything." |
| **FOMO + Controversy** | "Something is about to happen — and it shouldn't be allowed to." |

Stack by opening with one type and pivoting to the second within the first 5 seconds.

## Delivery Notes

These apply regardless of hook type:

- **State the claim as fact.** Never use "maybe," "I think," "you could argue." Hedging kills the hook's authority before the first sentence ends.
- **No intro.** No "hey guys," no "welcome back." The hook IS the first thing the viewer hears.
- **Pace matches emotion.** Shock and FOMO move fast. Nostalgia breathes. Contrarian is measured and confident. Match your voice to the feeling.
- **The hook buys you 5–8 seconds.** The next beat must deepen the promise — don't stall, don't summarise. Deliver the first piece of evidence or the first narrative escalation immediately after the hook.
